What Drives Me

I’ve spent a decade in work that puts me in front of people: behind an espresso bar, on a sales floor, in the back of a 7 Eleven fixing a planogram. What I’ve learned is that I do my best work when it means something, when I’m trusted to find the best way to get there, and when the people around me are better off for it. This is the short version of what motivates me.

01

What Motivates Me

  • Empathy

    I like understanding where someone is actually coming from. It’s what made me an effective barista trainer and a better salesperson than the pitch-first approach ever did.

  • Learning

    I’m happiest when I’m picking something up I didn’t know a month ago, whether that’s route optimization math, Salesforce, or how a roaster thinks about sourcing.

  • Creativity

    I want to find a better way to do a thing. The seasonal drink programs, the product concept I submitted through the innovation portal, the territory analytics I built in Excel to make our route data usable. That is the part of the job I look forward to most.

  • Fun

    A relaxed room is a productive room. I try to bring some levity to wherever I’m working, because people who are at ease communicate more openly, and that makes the work better.

  • Impact

    I need to see that the work moved something. A 54% drop in drive distance. A café’s sales up 15%. Regulars who kept coming back after I left. If I can’t point to the change yet, I keep working until I can.

  • Service

    I get real satisfaction from making someone else’s day easier, whether that’s a store manager, a teammate, or a customer at the counter. Most of what I’m proud of comes down to someone leaving better than they arrived.

  • Purpose

    I want to be part of something bigger than my own to-do list. The best jobs I’ve had were ones where I believed in what the place was trying to be.

02

What I Value

My core values, in the order I’d rank them.

  1. 1

    Self-direction

    I do my best work with clear expectations and the room to work out how to meet them. Give me the goal and I’ll come back with a plan.

  2. 2

    Benevolence

    Caring about the people right in front of me: the team, the store manager, the regular at the counter.

  3. 3

    Universalism

    Wanting things to be fair and good beyond my own circle, not just inside it.

  4. 4

    Stimulation

    I’m at my best with variety and a new problem to solve. When a process is running smoothly, I start looking for what could run better.

03

How I Work Best

I think of myself as a structured creative. I don’t just want to make something new, I want to build it into a system that holds up when I’m not there: a training program, a route model, a seasonal menu that becomes a permanent one.

I do my most focused thinking in a quiet space, and I bring that preparation into every store visit and client conversation. I work well with managers who set clear expectations and give me room to meet them, and I make a point of keeping them informed along the way.

I’m energized by putting original ideas into practice, and I’ve learned to build them inside the systems and standards a business already runs on. That’s how an idea becomes something that lasts after I’ve moved on to the next problem.
